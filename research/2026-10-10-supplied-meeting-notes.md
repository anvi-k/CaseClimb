Captured October 10, 2026. Source: notes supplied by the user with the research request. Wording is preserved with normalized Markdown formatting. ## Scope

- Narrow the problem down. For example, focus on the ED instead of the whole hospital (predicting number of admissions, etc.).
- Open question: what are Track 1 and Track 2? The digital patient encounter is one of them.

## Why it's valuable

- Efficiency: multiple students can practice at once.
- Trains empathy, even when the patient is being frustrating.
- The best doctors know you well enough to know what you'll respond to.

## Patient behavior

- Patients lie, so students can't just ask directly (e.g. “what are your vitamin C levels?”).
- Students need to probe indirectly with related questions:
  - “Tell me about your last dentist visit.”
  - “What brand of vitamins do you use?”
- Think through what a patient who is resistant to change looks like versus one who is open to it.
- Keep each patient session to 10 minutes for now.

## Scoring and feedback

- Rapport score: track the increase over the session.
- Empathy score: the student has to look at the avatar's face.
- Clinical alliance: assess it as part of the encounter.
- LLM debrief: instead of just listing what the student did wrong, point out where they needed more critical thinking.
  - Flag information they didn't probe for, and tell them when they aren't probing enough.
  - Call out moments where the patient lied and the student should have caught it.

## Avatar

- Use ElevenLabs and integrate it so students see a patient avatar.

## Faculty side

- Option to ramp up a patient's grumpiness.
- Adjustable patient mood, with some flexibility per patient.
- Goals set for each student.
- Practice for hard conversations, such as delivering a cancer diagnosis. Consider making “giving hard news” part of the process.
